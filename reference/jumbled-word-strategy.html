The C.L.U.E. Strategy

A repeatable way to decode any jumbled word — instead of guessing letter orders at random. Use it in this order, every time.

C
Context first Read the whole sentence before looking hard at the jumble. What kind of word is missing — a thing, an action, a feeling? The sentence usually tells you more than the letters do.
L
Letter chunks Scan the jumbled letters for familiar chunks rather than single letters. See the table below — spotting -TION or UN- inside a jumble instantly rules out most wrong orders.
U
Un-jumble by writing, not staring Write the letters out and physically try new orders (or circle chunks you found in step L). Staring at the original order rarely works — moving the letters does.
E
Errand-check Drop your answer back into the sentence and read it aloud in your head. Does it actually make sense? This catches near-miss words that use the right letters but aren't the answer.

Common letter chunks to watch for

ChunkWhere it shows up
UN-start of a word, usually means "not" (unhappy)
RE-start of a word, usually means "again" (rebuild)
-ING, -EDend of an action word
-TION, -SIONend of a word describing a thing/idea
TH, CH, SH, QUvery common letter pairs — rarely split apart
Double letters (SS, EE, OO)often stay next to each other
Why this works: the more anagrams you decode this way, the faster your eye starts spotting chunks automatically — that's the real skill, not memorising individual answers. Source: Twinkl — Twenty Amazing Anagram Puzzles, 11PlusEHelp — 11+ Jumbled Words.
